If you searched "outsourced CIO services" and landed here from a CFO's chair, you probably noticed the results are a mess. Search engines mix two entirely different industries under this term.

One set of results (BlackRock, Russell Investments, Commonfund) describes Outsourced Chief Investment Officer (OCIO) services for institutional investors. That's investment portfolio management for pension funds, endowments, and nonprofits. It has nothing to do with your technology stack.

The other meaning, the one you're likely after, is outsourced Chief Information Officer services: a firm that provides technology leadership, I.T. strategy, and infrastructure oversight for a company that doesn't have (or doesn't need) a full-time CIO on payroll. This article is about that second kind.

The confusion matters because it makes it harder to find straight answers on scope and pricing. Most firms that sell this kind of outsourced I.T. leadership don't publish their cost structure at all. This breaks that pattern.

## What Outsourced CIO Services Cover

An outsourced (or virtual) CIO does the work a full-time CIO would do, sized to a company that doesn't have one. That typically breaks into four buckets:

**Technology strategy and roadmap.** Mapping your technology plan to your business plan: what you need to invest in this year, next year, and in three years, tied to headcount growth, new locations, or new systems you're evaluating.

**Budget ownership and vendor management.** Building the annual I.T. budget, benchmarking it against what similar companies spend, and managing the relationships with your internet provider, software vendors, phone system, and hardware suppliers so you're not doing it yourself.

**Risk and cybersecurity oversight.** Making sure security isn't an afterthought: patching, backups, access controls, and a plan for compliance or cyber insurance requirements.

**Day-to-day I.T. management oversight.** Even if the outsourced CIO isn't the one fixing a printer, they're accountable for whoever is, whether that's an internal helpdesk person, a separate managed service provider, or the same firm's own technicians.

The difference between an outsourced CIO and a standard managed service provider (MSP) is the first two items. Straight managed I.T. gets your tickets closed and your servers patched. An outsourced CIO relationship adds the budget, roadmap, and business-outcome layer on top.

## The Three Ways Outsourced CIO Services Get Priced

Vendors in this space price the engagement one of three ways. Each has trade-offs a CFO should weigh before signing.

**Flat monthly retainer.** A fixed fee covering a set number of strategy hours, budget reviews, and vendor oversight per month, usually bundled with managed I.T. support. This is the most common model for companies under 150 employees because it's predictable: the same line item every month, easy to forecast against the annual budget.

**Per-user or per-device pricing.** The CIO strategy fee is layered on top of a per-seat managed services rate. This scales cleanly as you add headcount, but it means your CIO-level costs grow every time you hire, even if your technology roadmap hasn't changed.

**Hourly or project-based advisory.** Some firms sell virtual CIO time by the hour or by project (a network redesign, a due diligence review before an acquisition), with no ongoing retainer. This works for a company that needs a specific answer once, but it doesn't give you an accountable partner watching your systems between projects.

Most small and midsized businesses in Chicagoland end up in the first model, blended with managed I.T., because it avoids surprise invoices and keeps one vendor accountable for both strategy and execution.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down

Scope questions that change the number on your quote:

- **Number of employees and devices.** This is the single biggest driver of the managed I.T. portion of the bill.
- **Number of locations.** Multi-site businesses in the north suburbs (Northbrook, Glenview, Wheeling, Deerfield, Highland Park) often pay a per-location premium for network and connectivity oversight.
- **Compliance requirements.** HIPAA, PCI, or cyber insurance requirements add audit prep, documentation, and stricter access controls, which adds cost.
- **Current state of your infrastructure.** A company migrating off old, unsupported systems pays more in the first 90 days than a company with a clean environment.
- **How much of the work is bundled vs. separate.** A single vendor providing both managed I.T. and CIO strategy is usually priced lower in total than paying two separate firms for overlapping account management.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

A proposal that only shows a monthly number tells you nothing about what you're buying. Before signing, get written answers to:

- How many hours per month of CIO-level strategy time are included, separate from ticket resolution?
- Who owns the annual budget conversation, and how often does it happen?
- What's the response time commitment for security incidents, and is it local?
- Is cybersecurity oversight included in the base fee, or billed separately as incidents happen?
- What happens to pricing as you add employees or open a new location?
- Can they show you a sample technology roadmap document instead of a sales deck?

## Why This Matters More Without an I.T. Department

If your company has no internal I.T. staff, the outsourced CIO carries that accountability alone: whether your technology spend matches your business plan. Without that role filled, decisions get made ad hoc. A manager buys software without checking it against the rest of the stack, a security gap goes unnoticed until an incident forces the question, and nobody owns the budget conversation until it's an emergency.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between an outsourced CIO and a managed service provider (MSP)?** An MSP keeps your systems running: patching, monitoring, helpdesk tickets. An outsourced CIO does that plus owns the technology budget, builds a multi-year roadmap tied to your business plan, and manages vendor relationships. Many firms, including CIO Landing, provide both under one contract.

**Is outsourced CIO pricing usually a flat fee or hourly?** Most small and midsized businesses use a flat monthly retainer bundled with managed I.T. support, because it's predictable for budgeting. Hourly or project-based advisory exists but works better for a one-time question than an ongoing accountable relationship.

**Does outsourced CIO service include cybersecurity?** It should. Risk and cybersecurity oversight (patching, backups, access controls, compliance documentation) is a core part of the role, not an add-on. If a vendor's proposal treats security as separate and optional, ask why.

**How many employees do you need before an outsourced CIO makes sense?** There's no fixed headcount cutoff. The real signal is whether anyone internally owns the technology budget and roadmap conversation. Companies with no in-house I.T. department, from a handful of employees to a few hundred, use outsourced CIO services for that reason.
