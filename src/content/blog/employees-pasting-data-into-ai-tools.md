AI tools at work create a new kind of data leak, and most owners do not know it is happening. Your team has discovered ChatGPT and similar tools. They use them to write emails, summarize documents, clean up spreadsheets, and answer questions. The work gets faster. The problem is what they paste in to get that result.

To summarize a contract, someone pastes the contract. To fix a spreadsheet, someone pastes the numbers. To draft a reply to a client, someone pastes the client thread. Customer names, pricing, financials, employee records, all of it goes into a tool you do not control, on an account you do not manage, under terms you have not read.

Most of the time nothing bad happens. The risk is that you have no record of what left the building. If a client asks how you protect their data, "I think my staff are careful" is not an answer. And some free AI accounts reserve the right to use what you type to train their models. Your pricing could end up shaping an answer someone else receives.

Banning AI is the wrong move. It sends people to their phones and personal accounts, where you have even less visibility. The better path is to make the safe way the easy way.

A few practical steps. Pick an approved tool and pay for the business tier, where your data is not used for training and you get an admin view. Tell your team plainly what they can and cannot paste. Client data, financials, and anything with personal information stays out unless the tool is approved for it. Give them examples, because "be careful" is too vague to follow. Most people want to do the right thing once they know where the line is.

At CIO Landing we walk clients through this exact setup. Which tool, which tier, what the rules are, and how to turn off model training where the option exists. It is a short conversation that closes a gap most companies do not know is open.

AI at work is here to stay, and the productivity gain is large enough that fighting it is a losing move. The job is to channel it. Give people a tool you have vetted, a clear line they understand, and the result is faster work without the quiet leak.

Ask yourself one thing this week. If I could see every document my team pasted into an AI tool last month, would I be comfortable with the list?
